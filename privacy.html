Privacy policy

shortbot · last updated 6 September 2026

Summary

shortbot is a single-user program operated by one person on their own hardware. It authenticates only as its operator and only to that operator's own YouTube channel. It collects no data from any other person, and it has no other users.

Who operates it

One individual, running it on a personal machine. Contact: shortbot11@gmail.com.

It is two programs, and that is deliberate

shortbot is split into two programs that run separately and hold separate credentials:

  • The generator writes scripts, fetches stock footage, synthesises narration and assembles a video into a folder. It is the part that uses a language model. It holds no upload permission and cannot publish anything.
  • The uploader takes one finished video from that folder and publishes it. It contains no language model and makes no decisions — it checks a stop file, checks a rate limit, verifies the video, uploads, and confirms the result.
This split is the main safety property of the design. The component that could behave unexpectedly has no ability to publish, and the component that publishes has no intelligence to behave unexpectedly with. It is enforced with separate OAuth credentials, not merely by keeping the code in different files.

What Google data it accesses, and why

Only the uploader authenticates to Google. It requests two scopes, against the operator's own account:

  • youtube.upload — to publish a video to the operator's own channel.
  • youtube.readonly — to read that video's status back after upload and confirm it actually published, rather than trusting the upload response.

Both are scoped to the operator's own channel. The program does not access other channels, other accounts, or any Google service beyond YouTube.

Two further capabilities are planned — reading the channel's own analytics, and reading and replying to comments on its own videos. Neither is built, and no permission for either is registered on the consent screen or requested by any code. The consent screen declares exactly the two scopes above and nothing else. If those components are built, the additional permission will be requested and this policy updated before they run.

What it stores

  • An OAuth token, on the operator's own machine, so the uploader can publish without re-authenticating daily. It is not transmitted anywhere except to Google.
  • Its own videos and their metadata — scripts, titles, settings used, and later the view figures for those videos — in a local database, so it can compare what worked.

What it does not do

  • No analytics, tracking, cookies, or telemetry on this website.
  • No selling, sharing, or transferring of data to anyone.
  • No advertising and no advertising identifiers.
  • No accounts, no sign-in, and no collection of anything from visitors to this page.
  • No access to any Google account other than the operator's own.

Third parties

Video clips come from stock libraries (Pexels and Pixabay) under their licences, and every clip is credited to both the platform and the individual contributor in the video description. Text is generated using Google's Gemini API. Speech is synthesised locally, on the operator's own machine. None of these receive personal data about anyone other than, unavoidably, the operator as the account holder.

Retention and deletion

Data lives on the operator's machine and can be deleted by deleting it. The OAuth token can be revoked at any time from Google account permissions, which immediately ends the program's access.

Changes

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it.
